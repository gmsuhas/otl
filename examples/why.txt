Why otl

---
Who it is for

Someone who wants to see what a trace actually is before installing an SDK, a collector, and a backend. The input is a text file. The output is either a few indented lines or one JSON document.

---
How it helps

OpenTelemetry has a lot of surface: APIs, SDKs, protobuf, gRPC, HTTP, three signals, and a collector in the middle. Most of the tutorials start in the middle of that. otl starts from the event. A start and a stop become a span. A span opened inside another becomes a child. A failure becomes a status. Then the same tree is written in the JSON shape the spec describes. The ids and the enums match, and a date turns the time of day into unix time, which is the part a backend can place.

That is small enough to read in an afternoon, and small enough to throw away once the real SDK is the right tool.

---
What it leaves out

No metrics, no log signal, no sampling, no retries, no protobuf. It will not attach to a running Erlang node, and it will not replace the official OpenTelemetry SDK. The beam-telemetry library this started from already points at opentelemetry_telemetry for that job. posit-dev/picotel and dimaqq/otlp-json already send or encode real spans. This one is the picture of the conversion, not another client.
